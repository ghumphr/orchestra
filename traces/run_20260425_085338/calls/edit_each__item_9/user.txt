## reasoning_enhanced_inference.md
A major trend in 2025 is the decoupling of "knowledge acquisition" (pre-training) from "reasoning capability" (inference-time compute). Previously, a model's ability to reason was seen as a direct function of its parameter count. However, recent breakthroughs have demonstrated that by allocating more computational resources during the *inference* phase, even relatively small models can solve extremely complex logical and mathematical problems.

This trend is often referred to as "System 2 Thinking" for AI, drawing an analogy to Daniel Kahneman's psychological framework. While standard LLM generation is "System 1" (fast, instinctive, and probabilistic), reasoning-enhanced inference mimics "System 2" (slow, deliberate, and logical). This is achieved through several techniques, most notably "Chain-of-Thought" (CoT) prompting, "Tree-of-Thoughts" (ToT) search, and "Self-Consistency" decoding. Instead of predicting the next token immediately, the model is encouraged to generate an internal "scratchpad" of reasoning steps, exploring multiple potential paths of logic.

Technically, this is implemented through search algorithms like Monte Carlo Tree Search (MCTS) integrated with the language model. The model treats the generation process as a search problem through a space of possible logical deductions. At each step, it evaluates the "value" or "correctness" of a reasoning path, pruning unsuccessful branches and doubling down on promising ones. This allows the model to "think" longer about harder questions. This paradigm shift means that the future of AI performance will not just be about scaling the number of weights, but about scaling the amount of compute used during the decision-making process itself.
