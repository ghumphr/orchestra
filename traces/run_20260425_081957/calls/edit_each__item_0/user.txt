## agentic_autonomous_workflows.md
The focus of AI development has transitioned from single-turn chat interfaces to autonomous, agentic workflows. In 2025, the "Agentic Turn" describes the ability of models to operate as independent entities capable of planning, using tools, and correcting their own mistakes. Instead of providing a direct answer, an agentic system breaks a complex goal into a series of sub-tasks, selects appropriate software tools (APIs, web browsers, code interpreters), and executes them in a loop.

Technically, this is supported by advancements in "Reasoning and Acting" (ReAct) frameworks and multi-agent orchestration. Models are now trained specifically on "trajectory data"—sequences of thoughts, actions, and observations—to improve their planning capabilities. Advanced architectures incorporate "Self-Correction" loops, where a second "critic" model or a formal verification step checks the output of the primary "actor" model, triggering a re-planning cycle if errors are detected.

Multi-agent systems (MAS) have also become a standard in enterprise deployment. In these systems, specialized agents (e.g., a "Coder," a "Reviewer," and a "Manager") collaborate to solve problems, mimicking human organizational structures. This modularity allows for greater scalability and robustness, as the failure of a single agent can be mitigated by the collective oversight of the group. The frontier of this research lies in "Long-Horizon Planning," where agents can maintain goal-consistency over days or weeks of autonomous operation.
