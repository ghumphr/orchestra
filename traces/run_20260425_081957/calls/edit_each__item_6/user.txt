## native_multimodality_omni_models.md
The era of "stitched" multimodal models—where separate encoders for vision, audio, and text are glued to a language model—has ended. It has been replaced by the rise of Native Multimodal (Omni) Models. These architectures are trained from the ground up on a unified token stream, where audio, video, images, and text are all projected into a single, cohesive latent space. This allows the model to exhibit "cross-modal reasoning," such as understanding the emotional nuance in a speaker's tone or the physical implications of a visual gesture in real-time.

The technical breakthrough lies in the development of universal tokenizers. Instead of using a pre-trained CLIP encoder for images, these models use unified patch-based tokenization that can handle varying resolutions and temporal scales. This end-to-end training ensures that the model's internal representations are not biased by the limitations of a secondary encoder, leading to much higher semantic alignment across modalities.

This native integration is most evident in "low-latency omni-models" capable of real-time human-AI interaction. Because the model processes all inputs through a single backbone, it can respond to visual and auditory cues with the same immediacy as text, enabling fluid, natural conversations. This is a critical step toward achieving Artificial General Intelligence (AGI), as it mimics the sensory integration seen in biological organisms.
